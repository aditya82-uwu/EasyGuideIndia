The Reserve Bank of India’s **Authentication Mechanisms for Digital Payment Transactions Directions, 2025**, issued on 25 September 2025, apply from **1 April 2026**. They set out how banks and payment companies must verify that it’s really you making a payment. Here’s what changed, in plain language.

## The key rules

-   **Two-factor authentication (2FA)** remains required for domestic digital payments, unless a transaction type is specifically exempted.
-   The two factors must come from different categories: something you **know** (PIN, password), something you **have** (your phone or a device-bound token) or something you **are** (fingerprint, face).
-   For most transactions, **at least one factor must be dynamic**, meaning it’s unique to each transaction (such as an OTP or a cryptographic token).
-   Payment providers can now use options beyond SMS OTP, such as **app-based authentication, device binding, passkeys and biometrics**. SMS OTP is still allowed.
-   Providers can apply extra, **risk-based checks** on unusual or high-risk transactions.

## What this means for you

-   You may see more **in-app approvals**, fingerprint or face prompts, instead of SMS OTPs.
-   Unusual transactions may need extra verification.
-   Never share OTPs, PINs or approval prompts. Legitimate banks never ask for them.

## If you’re a fraud victim

1.  Block your card / UPI immediately through your bank’s app or helpline.
2.  Report cyber fraud on **1930** or at [cybercrime.gov.in](https://cybercrime.gov.in/).
3.  Inform your bank in writing as quickly as possible. RBI’s customer liability rules limit your loss if you report unauthorised transactions quickly.
