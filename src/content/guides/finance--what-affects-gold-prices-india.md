**Gold prices in India** depend on three things: the international price (set mainly on COMEX and in London), the **rupee–dollar exchange rate**, and **taxes** (import duty and GST). Local demand during weddings and festivals adds a smaller effect. This guide explains each factor, why prices rise or fall, and how gold compares with silver and fixed deposits.

## 1\. International gold price

India imports almost all its gold, so the domestic price follows the global price, quoted in US dollars per ounce. The global price moves with:

-   **US interest rates:** gold pays no interest, so when rates rise, holding gold costs more in lost interest and prices tend to fall. When rates are cut, gold usually gains.
-   **The US dollar:** a stronger dollar usually pushes gold down, and a weaker one pushes it up.
-   **Geopolitical risk:** wars and crises (such as the 2026 Middle East conflict and the disruption in the Strait of Hormuz) send investors to gold as a safe haven.
-   **Central bank buying:** central banks, including the RBI, have been large gold buyers in recent years.
-   **Profit-booking:** after a sharp rally, investors often sell, which causes short dips.

More on the benchmark in [what is COMEX gold and silver](/finance/what-is-comex-gold-silver/).

## 2\. The rupee

Gold is paid for in dollars. If the rupee weakens from, say, ₹85 to ₹90 per dollar, gold gets about 6% more expensive in India even if the global price hasn’t moved.

## 3\. Import duty and GST

-   **Import duty:** cut to 6% in July 2024, then raised to **15% on 13 May 2026** to reduce gold imports and support the rupee, according to the World Gold Council. A duty change moves Indian prices immediately.
-   **GST:** 3% on the value of gold, plus 5% on making charges for jewellery.

Budget 2026-27 (February 2026) did not change the duty on gold. The 2026 increase came later, as a separate step.

## 4\. Local demand

Demand peaks around Akshaya Tritiya, Dhanteras/Diwali and the wedding season. Jewellers may then charge a premium over the benchmark rate. When prices are very high, demand falls and dealers sometimes offer discounts.

## Where to check today’s gold rate

For a reliable reference rate, use the **IBJA** (India Bullion and Jewellers Association) daily rates, or the MCX gold futures price. Jewellers’ rates include their margin and vary by city. Always check the **BIS hallmark and HUID** before buying jewellery: see [how to verify gold hallmarking](/documents/verify-gold-hallmarking-online/).

## Ways to invest in gold

| Option | Regulated by | Points to know |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Jewellery | BIS (hallmarking) | Making charges and GST are lost when you sell |
| Coins and bars | BIS (hallmarking) | Buy hallmarked. Banks sell coins but generally don’t buy them back |
| Gold ETFs | SEBI | Needs a demat account, low cost, trades like a share |
| Gold mutual funds | SEBI | Invest in gold ETFs. No demat needed, SIP possible |
| Sovereign Gold Bonds | RBI | No new issues are being made. Existing bonds trade on exchanges |
| “Digital gold” apps | Not regulated by SEBI or RBI | SEBI has cautioned investors that these products fall outside its oversight |

## Gold vs silver

-   **Silver** is also an industrial metal (solar panels, electronics), so it moves more sharply in both directions than gold.
-   **Gold** is the steadier safe-haven asset and is bought heavily by central banks.
-   Many investors keep a small allocation to precious metals, mainly gold, and treat silver as a higher-risk addition.

## Gold vs fixed deposit

-   An **FD** gives a fixed, known return, and deposits up to ₹5 lakh per bank are insured by DICGC. Interest is taxed at your slab rate.
-   **Gold** pays no interest. Its return comes only from price changes, which can be large in either direction over short periods.
-   They do different jobs: an FD for certainty and emergency money, gold as a long-term hedge against inflation, a weaker rupee and crises.
