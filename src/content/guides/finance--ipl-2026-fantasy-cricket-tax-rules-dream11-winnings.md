**Short answer:** since August 2025, paid fantasy cricket contests (the kind where you pay an entry fee to win cash) are **banned in India** under the **Promotion and Regulation of Online Gaming Act, 2025**. Dream11 and similar apps stopped paid contests and moved to free-to-play formats. Any winnings you did earn, including older winnings still to be reported, and cash prizes from free contests, are taxed at a flat **30%** plus cess.

## What the Online Gaming Act, 2025 changed

-   It bans offering, operating or advertising **online money games**, whether they’re called games of skill or games of chance. Paid fantasy sports are included.
-   Banks and payment apps may not process payments for such games.
-   It promotes **e-sports** and **online social games** (free, or subscription-based without stakes).
-   Penalties for operators run up to three years in jail and a fine of up to ₹1 crore.

After the Act passed in August 2025, **Dream11 paused all paid contests** and moved to free-to-play and other sports products. Other fantasy apps did the same. Be careful of any app or website still offering paid cash contests: it is operating illegally, and your money isn’t protected.

## How winnings are taxed

| Type of winnings | Tax rate | TDS |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Online games (net winnings), until paid games stopped | 30% flat + 4% cess (plus surcharge if applicable) | 30% on net winnings, no minimum threshold |
| Prizes from lotteries, game shows, contests (including free contests with cash prizes) | 30% flat + cess | 30% if the prize exceeds ₹10,000 |

-   **Net winnings** = amount withdrawn (or held at year-end) minus deposits, calculated over the year.
-   **No basic exemption:** the 30% applies even if your total income is below the taxable limit, and the ₹12 lakh rebate doesn’t cover it.
-   **No deductions or loss set-off:** you can’t claim expenses or set off losses from other games against these winnings.
-   The Income-tax Act, 2025 (in force from 1 April 2026) continues to tax such winnings at a flat 30%.

## Worked example

Say you deposited ₹5,000 during a year and withdrew ₹25,000 in winnings from a fantasy app. Net winnings = ₹20,000. Tax at 30% = ₹6,000, plus 4% cess = **₹6,240**. This would have been deducted as TDS by the platform when you withdrew.

## Reporting in your ITR

1.  Download your **Form 26AS / AIS** from the Income Tax e-filing portal. TDS deducted by the gaming platform shows there.
2.  Report the winnings under **Income from Other Sources**, in the special-rate section.
3.  Claim the TDS already deducted. Tax at 30% is usually fully covered, so there’s normally nothing more to pay.

See [how to calculate income tax](/finance/how-to-calculate-income-tax-india/) for how this fits with your other income.

## What about money stuck in a fantasy app wallet?

After the ban, the major platforms announced that users could withdraw their deposit and winnings balances. If you still have a balance, use the app’s withdrawal or support option, and keep records of deposits and withdrawals for your tax return.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is Dream11 legal now?

Dream11 now offers free-to-play formats, which are allowed. Paid cash contests are banned under the Online Gaming Act, 2025.

### Are free fantasy contest prizes taxable?

Yes. Cash prizes (and the value of prizes in kind) are taxed at 30%, with TDS if the prize exceeds ₹10,000.

### I won money before the ban but didn’t report it. What now?

Report it in the return for that year. If the deadline has passed, you can file an **updated return (ITR-U)** by paying additional tax. TDS already deducted still counts.
