How much you get back when you cancel a train ticket depends on **when you cancel** and whether the ticket is **confirmed, RAC or waitlisted**. Indian Railways tightened the rules in 2026, so cancelling late now costs more. Here’s the current structure and how to get your money back.

## Confirmed tickets: refund by cancellation time

| Cancelled before departure | Deduction |
| --- | --- |
| More than 72 hours | Only a flat cancellation charge per passenger (depends on class) |
| 72 to 24 hours | 25% of the fare (subject to the flat minimum) |
| 24 to 8 hours | 50% of the fare (subject to the flat minimum) |
| Less than 8 hours | No refund |

These windows follow news reports of the revised rules from April 2026, which moved the no-refund cut-off from 4 hours to 8 hours. We could not match every slab to a Railway Board notice, so rely on the amount IRCTC shows before you confirm a cancellation.

## RAC and waitlisted tickets

-   A small clerkage charge is deducted when you cancel an RAC or waitlisted ticket before the cut-off.
-   An e-ticket that stays **fully waitlisted after chart preparation** is **cancelled automatically** with a refund (minus clerkage).

## Tatkal tickets

**Confirmed [Tatkal](/railway/irctc-tatkal-ticket-booking/) tickets are not refundable.** Waitlisted Tatkal tickets follow the waitlist rules.

## When you get a full refund

-   The train is **cancelled** by Railways
-   The train is late by more than 3 hours and you don’t travel (file a **TDR** before departure)
-   Other cases covered by TDR, such as AC failure or travelling in a lower class

## How refunds are paid

For online e-tickets, the refund goes back to the original payment method, usually within a few working days. For counter tickets, collect it at the counter as per the rules.
