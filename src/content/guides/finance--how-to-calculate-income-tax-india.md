To work out your **income tax in India**, add up your income, subtract the deductions your tax regime allows, apply the slab rates, then subtract any rebate and add 4% cess. This guide walks through each step with worked examples, using the rates for **FY 2026-27** (the tax year starting 1 April 2026).

## What changed in 2026

-   **New law:** the **Income-tax Act, 2025** replaced the Income-tax Act, 1961 from **1 April 2026**. Section numbers and form names are changing, but the way tax is calculated for most people is the same.
-   **Rates unchanged:** Budget 2026-27 (1 February 2026) did not change slab rates, the standard deduction or the rebate under either regime.
-   **Other Budget 2026 changes:** TCS on overseas tour packages cut to 2%; TCS on foreign remittances for education and medical treatment cut from 5% to 2%; interest awarded by a Motor Accident Claims Tribunal is now tax-free; STT on futures and options was raised; share buybacks are taxed as capital gains for all shareholders.

## Step 1: Add up your total income

Income is grouped under five heads:

1.  **Salary** (including pension)
2.  **House property** (rent received, or interest on a home loan)
3.  **Business or profession**
4.  **Capital gains** (sale of shares, mutual funds, property, gold)
5.  **Other sources** (bank and FD interest, dividends, gifts above the limit)

Most capital gains and some winnings are taxed at **special rates**, not slab rates, so keep them aside when you apply the slabs.

## Step 2: Choose your regime

The **new regime** is the default. It has lower rates but allows very few deductions. The **old regime** has higher rates but lets you claim deductions such as 80C, 80D and HRA. Salaried people can choose each year when filing the return. See our comparison: [old vs new tax regime](/finance/).

## Step 3: Apply the slab rates

### New tax regime (FY 2026-27)

| Taxable income | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹4,00,000 | Nil |
| ₹4,00,001 – ₹8,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹8,00,001 – ₹12,00,000 | 10% |
| ₹12,00,001 – ₹16,00,000 | 15% |
| ₹16,00,001 – ₹20,00,000 | 20% |
| ₹20,00,001 – ₹24,00,000 | 25% |
| Above ₹24,00,000 | 30% |

-   **Standard deduction** for salary and pension: ₹75,000.
-   **Rebate:** up to ₹60,000, so there is **no tax if taxable income is up to ₹12 lakh** (₹12.75 lakh for a salaried person after the standard deduction). The rebate doesn’t apply to special-rate income such as capital gains.

### Old tax regime (below 60 years)

| Taxable income | Rate |
| --- | --- |
| Up to ₹2,50,000 | Nil |
| ₹2,50,001 – ₹5,00,000 | 5% |
| ₹5,00,001 – ₹10,00,000 | 20% |
| Above ₹10,00,000 | 30% |

The basic exemption is ₹3 lakh for senior citizens (60–79) and ₹5 lakh for super senior citizens (80+). The standard deduction is ₹50,000, and the rebate (up to ₹12,500) makes income up to ₹5 lakh tax-free.

## Step 4: Add surcharge and cess

-   **Health and education cess:** 4% of the tax (plus surcharge), for everyone who pays tax.
-   **Surcharge:** only if income exceeds ₹50 lakh (10% up to ₹1 crore, then higher). In the new regime the highest surcharge is capped at 25%.

## Worked examples (new regime, salaried)

### Salary ₹12,00,000

Taxable income = ₹12,00,000 − ₹75,000 = ₹11,25,000. Slab tax = ₹20,000 (5% of ₹4 lakh) + ₹32,500 (10% of ₹3.25 lakh) = ₹52,500. This is below ₹12 lakh, so the rebate cancels it. **Tax payable: nil.**

### Salary ₹18,00,000

Taxable income = ₹17,25,000. Slab tax = ₹20,000 + ₹40,000 (10% of ₹4 lakh) + ₹60,000 (15% of ₹4 lakh) + ₹25,000 (20% of ₹1.25 lakh) = ₹1,45,000. Add 4% cess (₹5,800). **Tax payable: ₹1,50,800.**

**Marginal relief:** if your income is just above ₹12 lakh, the tax you pay can’t be more than the income above ₹12 lakh. The Income Tax Department’s calculator applies this automatically.

## Income that is tax-free (fully or partly)

-   **Agricultural income** (it’s added to your income only to work out the rate on your other income, under the old regime).
-   **PPF** interest and maturity amount.
-   **EPF** withdrawal after 5 years of continuous service. Interest on your own contributions above ₹2.5 lakh a year is taxable.
-   **Gifts** from relatives (any amount), gifts on marriage, and gifts from others up to ₹50,000 in a year.
-   **Gratuity** up to ₹20 lakh for private-sector employees, and **leave encashment** at retirement up to ₹25 lakh (the full amount is exempt for government employees).
-   **Life insurance maturity**, subject to premium limits in the law.
-   **HRA** and **LTA** (old regime only, within limits).
-   **Interest from a Motor Accident Claims Tribunal award** (from FY 2026-27).

## Where to calculate and file

Use the free **tax calculator** on the Income Tax e-filing portal to check your figure, then file your return there. Your employer deducts TDS based on the regime you declare. Check that the TDS matches your **Form 26AS / AIS** before filing. Make sure your PAN is linked to Aadhaar: see [PAN–Aadhaar link status](/documents/check-pan-aadhaar-link-status/).
