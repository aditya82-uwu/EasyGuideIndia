There is no single exam or form for becoming a professional cricketer in India. Players move up through **club cricket, district teams, state age-group teams and then the senior state side**, and from there to IPL scouts and national selectors. This guide shows what to do at each age, including if you are starting late.

## The path in one view

| Age | What to do | BCCI tournament (men) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| About 10 to 13 | Join a club or academy affiliated to your district association | None yet |
| 14 to 15 | District trials and inter-district tournaments | Selection towards the under-16 team |
| 15 to 16 | State under-16 team | Vijay Merchant Trophy |
| 16 to 18 | State under-19 team | Cooch Behar Trophy (multi-day), Vinoo Mankad Trophy (one-day) |
| 19 to 22 | State under-23 team | CK Nayudu Trophy |
| Senior | State senior team | Ranji Trophy, Vijay Hazare Trophy, Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy |

The BCCI also runs a women's domestic calendar with under-15, under-19, under-23 and senior competitions. The state association route is the same.

## If you are 14 or younger

-   Join a **club or academy affiliated to your district association**. Selectors rarely pick players with no record in recognised matches.
-   Play as many official matches as you can, because your scores and wickets are recorded.
-   Keep your **birth certificate and school records** safe and make sure the dates match. Age proof is checked for age-group cricket.

## If you are 15 to 16

-   Attend **district trials**, usually held before the domestic season. Watch your district and state association's official website.
-   Strong performers are picked for inter-district tournaments, then a **state probables camp** with fitness tests.
-   The first big target is the **state under-16 team** for the Vijay Merchant Trophy.

## If you are 17 to 19

-   Move up to the **state under-19 team**. The BCCI sets a cut-off date for each season (usually 1 September), and you must be under 19 on that date. Check your association's circular for the exact date.
-   The **Cooch Behar Trophy** is the main multi-day tournament and the **Vinoo Mankad Trophy** the one-day one. Selectors and scouts watch both.

## If you are 20 to 23

-   The **state under-23 side** plays the CK Nayudu Trophy. It is the bridge to the senior team.
-   Keep your numbers consistent across a season. One big innings counts for less than steady performance.

## If you are 23 or older, or starting late

-   There is no age cut-off that we found for the senior Ranji Trophy, but the age-group tournaments have cut-offs, so older players compete through club leagues and senior state trials.
-   Play in your association's **top division club league** and in state T20 leagues such as the TNPL and the Maharaja Trophy, which scouts follow.
-   Ask your association when senior trials are held. You may also need **domicile or residence proof** for the state, or a no-objection certificate if you are moving from another association.

## Documents you will need

-   **Birth certificate** and school records with a matching date of birth.
-   **Aadhaar** with the same name and date of birth, and a **PAN** for contracts and payments.
-   **Bank account** in your name.
-   For age-group cricket, the BCCI's age-verification programme can include a **medical bone-age test**. Players found to have falsified age face bans, so never alter documents.

## What selectors look for

-   Consistent performance across a season, not one-off scores.
-   Fitness. State teams run fitness tests such as timed runs and the yo-yo test, and standards vary by association.
-   A clear role: batter, bowler, wicketkeeper or all-rounder.
-   Temperament, fielding and discipline.

## Beware of scams

Every season some people advertise paid "IPL trials" or "selection" on social media. Official BCCI, state and franchise selection does not work through paid public registration. Do not pay anyone who promises selection, and confirm trial dates on your state association's official website.

## From state cricket to the IPL and India

Performances in the Ranji Trophy, Vijay Hazare Trophy and especially the Syed Mushtaq Ali Trophy are closely watched by IPL scouts and national selectors. Our guides cover each step in more detail.
