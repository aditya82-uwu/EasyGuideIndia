A **share price** moves when more people want to buy a stock than sell it, or the other way round. What changes their minds falls into three groups: **the company’s own performance**, **its sector**, and **the wider economy and market mood**. This guide explains each group with examples from Indian stocks (banks, IT, power, consumer companies), so you can make sense of why a stock rose or fell.

## 1\. Company-specific factors

-   **Quarterly results:** revenue, profit and margins compared with what analysts expected. A stock can fall on good results if they were below expectations.
-   **Guidance and order book:** what management says about future growth, new orders or projects (important for power, infrastructure and capital-goods companies).
-   **Debt:** high debt makes a company sensitive to interest rates and credit-rating changes.
-   **Corporate actions:** dividends, bonus issues, stock splits, buybacks, mergers and demergers. From 2026, buybacks are taxed as capital gains in shareholders’ hands.
-   **Promoter activity:** promoters buying or selling shares, or pledging them for loans.
-   **Governance and news:** regulatory action, legal cases, auditor issues or management changes can move a stock sharply.

Listed companies must disclose material information to the exchanges. Check announcements on the **NSE** and **BSE** websites rather than social media tips.

## 2\. Sector factors

### Banking and financial stocks

-   RBI repo rate decisions and bond yields (which affect lending margins).
-   Credit growth and deposit growth.
-   Bad loans (NPAs) and provisions.

Because every bank faces the same drivers, **banking stocks often rise or fall together**. See [Sensex vs Nifty vs Bank Nifty](/finance/sensex-vs-nifty-vs-bank-nifty-difference/).

### IT stocks

-   **US and European demand:** most revenue of Indian IT companies comes from overseas clients, so a slowdown there hits deal wins.
-   **The rupee:** a weaker rupee raises rupee revenue, and a stronger one reduces it.
-   **AI and automation:** generative AI tools can shrink some traditional services work (testing, support, basic coding) while creating demand for AI projects and data-centre services. Markets re-price IT stocks as they judge which effect wins.
-   **Visa and trade policy** in the US.

### Power and energy

Electricity demand, fuel (coal, gas) costs, tariff orders from regulators, and capacity additions.

### Consumer (FMCG) companies

Rural and urban demand, raw material prices (milk, sugar, palm oil), GST changes and monsoon rainfall. Demergers, where a business is spun off into a separately listed company, also cause big price adjustments on the record date.

## 3\. Economy and market-wide factors

-   **Interest rates:** rate cuts usually support stocks. Rate hikes do the opposite.
-   **Inflation and GDP growth.**
-   **Foreign investor flows (FPIs):** heavy selling by foreign funds can drag the whole market down.
-   **The Union Budget:** tax changes (STT, capital gains) and spending plans move specific sectors. Budget 2026-27, for example, raised STT on futures and options.
-   **Crude oil and the rupee:** see [why crude oil prices rise or fall](/finance/why-crude-oil-prices-rise-or-fall/).
-   **Global events:** wars, US interest rates and sell-offs in other markets.

## Does Bitcoin affect Indian stocks?

Only indirectly. Crypto and stocks sometimes fall together when investors are avoiding risk globally, but Bitcoin is not part of any Indian index. In India, gains from crypto (virtual digital assets) are taxed at a flat **30%**, with 1% TDS on transfers. Losses can’t be set off against other income. Crypto is not legal tender and is not regulated by SEBI.

## How to check the facts behind a price move

1.  Read the company’s exchange filings (results, investor presentations, announcements) on NSE/BSE.
2.  Check if the whole sector or index moved the same way that day.
3.  Look for economy-wide news (RBI policy, Budget, global markets).
4.  Be wary of “target price” tips on social media and messaging apps. SEBI has warned investors about unregistered advisers.
